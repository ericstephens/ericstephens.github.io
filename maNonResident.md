# Introduction
This is a brief note on obtaining my MA Temporary Non-Resident License to Carry (LTC) permit.

# Disclaimer
Nothing here is even close to legal advice. If you go to prison because of something I wrote, not my problem. Oh, and establish dominance quickly if that happens :)

# BLUF
Despite a trip to western MA for a class and to Chelsea, MA for the "interview", obtaining the MA non-resident license to carry (LTC) was relatively easy and cost $100 for a course and $100 for the application fee. And gas money.

# License
MA may issue non-residents a temporary (1-year) license to carry (LTC). The renewal process shouldn't require another trip to MA (more on that later) but will require $100 each year. 

# Course
Applicants are required to take a ~4 hour course by a MA-endorsed instructor. I took mine at the Lenox Sportsman's Club (258 New Lenox Rd, Lenox MA) which was a 3-hour trip for me from Central Square, NY. $100 cash with a short quiz at the end like NY. Instructor was Tom Nadolny (tnadolny1@gmail.com) or see https://www.mass.gov/doc/basic-firearms-instructor-public-listing-08212026/download

The course (LTC-035) was conducted at the Lenox Sportsmans Club which is ~3 hours from Central NY. The class duration was 4 hours and consisted of lecture and a 25 question test for $100. Many people finished the test during the class as it was provided when we arrived. Anyone who has handled a firearm would've passed the test with some exceptions; there are MA-specific laws on use-of-force and safe storage which may vary from one's home state. In the case of NY residents who have done the 18-hour course, it was almost a no-brainer.

## Key takeaways (not exhaustive)
My notetaking was biased towards the differences between NY and MA. And also those things that would get me hung up if omitted or performed incorrectly

- Storage and use-of-force laws are mostly the same. MA is a duty to retreat state and firearms need to be secured when not in use or within arm's reach. Ironically, a locking glove box is considered safe storage.
- MA is *not* duty to inform. Like NY, when asked, one needs to answer truthfully. The question from law enforcement might be, "anything in the vehicle I should be concerned with?" which is the queue to disclose. 
- MA has a pistol roster for those items that are allows to be *purchased* in the state. According to the instructor, once licensed in MA one can carry whatever pistol they like (as long as it has low-capacity magazines).
- Pistols must be registered which is similar to NY.
- Included a hickok45 video 👍
- Pay attention to signage as they carry the weight of law. Government buildings are a no-go. Federal buildings too (even in the parking lot)
- Schools: not in the building but OK in the parking lot (properly secured)
- A school event off school grounds makes that property a school
- License is good for one year which just means you pay every year

# Application
I was able to do the entire process online. Non-residents need to pick the "Non-resident temporary LTC" which will "route" the application to MA's central Department of Criminal Justice Information Services (DCJIS). I screwed this up twice but the good news is re-submitting the application was a 5-10 minute process since it retained much of my data. Once the application is accepted, they will email you and request payment. 

Once payment is accepted, they will email again and allow you to select when you want your interview. The travel to Chelsea, MA is the biggest wrinkle in this process

# Interview
This is conducted in Chelsea, MA at DCJIS; they will tell you where. My interview was less than 30 minutes and was basically data verification followed by fingerprinting. 

# Permit arrival
My interview was conducted on the 8th and my permit was issued on the 16th arriving to Central Square, NY on the 25th. I think we can agree that is pretty quick.

Pistols carried in MA must be registered online at the same portal as the application. MA provides your license number online and you download a PIN. Those with pistols obtained outside NY that are not SAFE Act compliant *may* not be compatible. Check [M.G.L. c. 140, § 121(b)](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section121). Note: just because its SAFE Act compliant doesn't mean its good-to-go in MA. I recommend one double checks.
